Fearlessness, purity of heart, steadiness in spiritual understanding, generosity, self-guidance, sacrifice, study, discipline and straightforwardness are divine qualities.|Choose one quality to practise concretely today, such as keeping a promise or giving useful help.
Nonviolence, truthfulness, freedom from anger, renunciation, peace, avoidance of slander, compassion, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty and steadiness continue the list.|Before speaking about an absent person, ask whether your words are true, necessary and fair.
Vigour, forgiveness, firmness, cleanliness, absence of malice and freedom from excessive pride also belong to the divine disposition.|Practise firmness without cruelty and forgiveness without pretending that harm never occurred.
Hypocrisy, arrogance, self-importance, anger, harshness and ignorance are destructive qualities.|Use the list to examine one of your own reactions, not to diagnose everyone who disagrees with you.
The divine disposition leads toward freedom; the destructive disposition leads toward bondage. Krishna reassures Arjuna that he has the divine potential.|Encouragement can support growth. Recognise a strength while continuing to work on a weakness.
Krishna distinguishes divine and destructive tendencies in beings and now explains the latter more fully.|Read the categories as an ethical examination of tendencies, not permission to dehumanise people.
Those dominated by destructive tendencies do not understand what should be pursued or avoided; purity, good conduct and truth are absent.|Before a decision, identify what should be done, what should be avoided and why.
They claim the world has no truth, moral foundation or divine ground, arising only from mutual desire.|Study the view Krishna criticises without assuming every person with different metaphysical beliefs behaves destructively.
Holding this view, with damaged understanding, they engage in harmful deeds that threaten the world.|Judge a policy or habit by its real effects, especially on people with less power.
With insatiable desire, hypocrisy, pride and arrogance, they cling to misguided ideas and pursue impure aims.|Notice an idea you defend mainly because it protects a desire. Examine the incentive honestly.
They remain absorbed in limitless anxiety until death, treating enjoyment as the highest aim.|Ask whether a pursuit is bringing genuine fulfilment or only the next worry.
Bound by countless expectations and driven by desire and anger, they accumulate wealth through unjust means for enjoyment.|Refuse one unfair shortcut, even if it seems to promise faster progress.
Their inner speech says, “I gained this today; I will fulfil this desire; I have this wealth and will get more.”|Notice whether satisfaction is always postponed to the next acquisition. Set a reasonable stopping point.
They boast of defeating enemies and imagine themselves powerful, successful, happy and in control.|Check the story you tell after success. Include help received, limits and ethical responsibility.
They think themselves rich and high-born, without equal, and plan impressive sacrifices and gifts while blinded by ignorance.|Give or practise without turning generosity into proof of status.
Confused by many plans, caught in delusion and attached to pleasure, they descend into destructive consequences.|Simplify one overloaded plan and examine whether it serves a worthwhile purpose.
Self-conceited, stubborn and intoxicated by wealth and pride, they perform sacrifices only in name, without genuine discipline.|Ask whether a public gesture matches your private conduct.
Driven by ego, force, pride, craving and anger, they resent the divine presence in themselves and others.|Notice when anger makes you treat another person as worthless. Restore boundaries without contempt.
Krishna describes cruel, hateful conduct as leading through rebirth into destructive conditions.|Read this as a karmic warning about conduct, not authority to declare another person’s spiritual destiny.
Persisting in such births, they fail to reach Krishna and fall further into suffering.|Focus on interrupting a harmful pattern now rather than using destiny as an excuse to continue it.
Craving, anger and greed are three gates to self-destruction; therefore they should be relinquished.|Identify which of these is strongest in a current decision and delay action until you can assess it.
Freed from these three gates, a person acts for their true welfare and reaches the highest goal.|Replace a harmful impulse with one specific constructive act, not merely a promise to be better.
One who ignores sound scriptural guidance and acts only by desire finds neither fulfilment, happiness nor the highest goal.|Do not make appetite your only guide. Examine action through ethics, understanding and reliable guidance.
Therefore let shastra guide discernment about what should and should not be done; understand it and act appropriately.|Study guidance in context and ask questions where needed. Do not use an isolated line to bypass compassion or judgment.
