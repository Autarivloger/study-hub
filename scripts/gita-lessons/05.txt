Arjuna asks which is better: renouncing action or practising disciplined action. He wants a clear way to live.|When comparing paths, ask which suits your current responsibilities and preparation, not which sounds more impressive.
Krishna says both can lead to the highest good, but disciplined action is generally preferable to outward renunciation.|Continue responsible work while developing a freer motive; a dramatic change of outer life is not automatically spiritual progress.
The true renouncer neither hates nor craves. Freedom from the grip of opposites makes release easier.|Notice both compulsive pursuit and compulsive rejection. Either can keep an object at the centre of your mind.
Only the immature treat knowledge and action as completely separate; one established properly in either reaches the fruit of both.|Look for how understanding improves your action and how action tests your understanding.
The state reached through knowledge is also reached through yoga; one who sees their unity truly sees.|Avoid turning different disciplines into rival identities. Ask what genuine transformation each requires.
Renunciation is difficult without yoga; the disciplined contemplative reaches Brahman more readily.|Build steadiness through daily practice before assuming that abandoning duties will produce inner freedom.
The integrated person is purified, self-guided and sees the self in all beings; though acting, such a person is not stained.|Bring equal respect into one interaction while still carrying out the practical task responsibly.
The knower understands, “I am not independently doing anything,” even while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping or breathing.|Observe one ordinary activity as a coordinated process, rather than a possession of the ego alone.
Speaking, releasing, receiving, opening and closing the eyes continue; the senses operate among their objects.|Notice automatic bodily activity. Use the observation to soften self-importance, not to deny accountability.
One who offers actions to Brahman and gives up attachment remains unstained, like a lotus leaf untouched by water.|Enter a task fully while practising freedom from the need to claim every outcome.
Yogis act through body, mind, intellect and senses without attachment, for inner purification.|Choose one task as training in patience or honesty, not merely a way to obtain a result.
The disciplined person relinquishes possessiveness toward results and finds peace; the craving-driven person remains bound by desire for the fruit.|After finishing work, review it usefully, then stop repeatedly checking for praise or reward.
The self-controlled embodied person mentally renounces ownership of action and rests in the body’s “city of nine gates.”|Practise observing bodily and mental activity without treating every movement as a new story about your importance.
The Lord does not create people’s sense of doership, their actions or their attachment to results; nature operates.|Study the distinction between divine reality and conditioned behaviour. Do not blame God for a choice you can examine and improve.
The all-pervading Lord takes on neither anyone’s wrongdoing nor merit; knowledge covered by ignorance leaves beings confused.|Take responsibility for understanding and correcting conduct rather than seeking a way to transfer blame.
When knowledge destroys ignorance, it illuminates the highest reality like the sun.|Notice one mistaken assumption and correct it carefully. Clarity is more useful than protecting a familiar error.
Those whose intellect, self-understanding and commitment rest in the highest reach freedom, their impurities removed through knowledge.|Give study a direction: what mistaken identification or harmful attachment are you trying to understand?
The wise see with equal spiritual vision a learned humble brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and a person treated as an outcaste.|Offer dignity without ranking a person’s inherent worth by status, background, usefulness or appearance.
Those established in equality have already overcome the hold of conditioned existence; Brahman is flawless and equal.|Practise consistency in respect, including toward someone who offers you no advantage.
Established in Brahman, the wise person is not carried away by pleasant events or shattered by unpleasant ones.|Let good news and bad news inform action without letting either determine your entire identity.
One unattached to external contacts finds happiness within and, united with Brahman, experiences enduring fulfilment.|Spend a brief interval without adding stimulation. Notice whether simple presence is possible without immediately seeking the next input.
Pleasures born of contact have beginnings and endings and can become sources of suffering. The wise do not cling to them.|Enjoy something appropriate without demanding that it last forever or solve a deeper dissatisfaction.
One who can withstand the force of desire and anger during embodied life is integrated and happy.|Delay one impulsive response until you can choose it rather than merely discharge it.
The yogi whose happiness, delight and light are within becomes established in Brahman and reaches liberation.|Develop an inner practice that is not dependent on constant approval or entertainment.
Sages whose doubts are cut, whose minds are guided and who care for the welfare of all beings reach liberation.|Pair reflection with one concrete act of care. Inner work and concern for others belong together.
Those free from desire and anger, self-guided and knowing the self, approach liberation on every side.|Track a recurring trigger with patience. Repeated practice matters more than declaring yourself free of emotion.
The contemplative withdraws attention from external contacts, steadies the gaze and balances the movements of breath.|Read this with the next verse as a traditional meditation description. Use comfortable breathing rather than forced ratios or retention.
With senses, mind and intellect guided, directed toward liberation and free from gripping desire, fear and anger, the sage is inwardly free.|Begin a short comfortable meditation with a modest intention: notice and return, without forcing a special state.
Knowing Krishna as the recipient of sacrifice and austerity, Lord of all worlds and friend of all beings brings peace.|Let devotion include trust and goodwill toward beings, rather than only requests for personal success.
